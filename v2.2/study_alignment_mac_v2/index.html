Alignment Model — M2/M3 Offsets and M2 Scale in QUEST (Plan)¶

Branch: to be created from feature/pec_theta_v2. Analysis: October 2026, from the sync tables in logs/archive.

Status (2026-10-11)¶

Implemented on feature/alignment_mac_v2; user documentation in kinematics.md §2.3.

  • driver/kinematics_mac.py: the fit (numpy, analytic derivatives, Gauss–Newton from QUEST's rotation): 0.6 ms for 10 points on a desktop, against 20 ms for the study prototype. The control-tick correction is a closed form: about 6 µs per tick.
  • The Mechanical Alignment Correction switch (advanced_align_mac) is the A/B switch. On: the rotation and d2, d3 refitted unweighted at every sync, with align_m2_scale (%, default 1.0). When the points can't separate the offsets, they are held and the rotation is the existing weighted QUEST. Off: the existing QUEST, unchanged.
  • The earlier MAC model has been retired (copy in utility/legacy_mac.py for fits_extract.py). Pilot's AutoTune now calibrates the M2 scale from the current sync points. First calibration on jdm's mount: +0.335%.
  • utility/alignment_ab.py replays the archived sync tables: goto accuracy (leave-one-out) median 59.2′ (MAC off) → 9.6′ (on), better on 45 of 47 alignments. The two worse: 6 points at roll ~0 (greg, 9.4′ → 11.7′), and a narrow-roll alignment both models fit poorly (86′ → 88′).
  • Timed on the Pi Zero 2 W and nina01 (Cost, below): 15.8 ms and 2.7 ms per fit, 53 µs and 9 µs per tick.
  • Still to do: the live A/B on the sky (alignment RMS, first-goto error, drift with MAC on and off from the same syncs); the scale-or-sag test with the 300 mm.

Summary¶

Multi-Point Alignment has rarely reached a low RMS: 6–10 point alignments typically report 30–60′, and up to 130′. The residuals are not noise. Across every archived log, three terms explain most of them:

  • M2 and M3 zero offsets (or, equivalently with this data, the camera tilted on the head), fitted at each alignment: they change with the lens and between sessions.
  • An M2 scale of about +1–2%, the same on every Polaris in the logs: M2 turns 1–2% further than its reported angle says, over the altitudes used.

With these fitted together with QUEST's rotation, alignment residuals fall from a median of 36–95′ to 2–8′.

MAC does not model these terms correctly: its zero offsets are never applied, its strongest term (the roll-dependent m3_tilt_dm2) cannot be applied before QUEST and is switched off, and its M2 term is a sine that acts as a scale and offset, fitted through a pre-QUEST sign convention that QUEST must absorb. This plan replaces MAC's role with a joint fit.

The large drift rates PEC Drift Correction has been absorbing (e.g. M11, 10 Oct: Dec 20–28″/min, MAC off, QUEST 39–62′) are the pointing error changing along the target's path, so a better model should also shrink them.

Evidence¶

Data¶

Every QUEST refit logs its point table: observed RA/Dec/Az/Alt and the raw motor pose p_az/p_alt/p_roll (stored before worm, MAC or sync-guide corrections, SyncManager.standard_entry). 1,300 distinct points from 70 logs (jdm, Mark, Vyskocil, Greg; June–October 2026). An alignment is a log's largest table before its point count drops, without points already used by an earlier one.

Method¶

The driver's forward kinematics (theta_to_q: C = Rz(−θ1+90°) · Ry(−θ2−90°) · Rx(−θ3), boresight −z) rebuilt in numpy with optional terms: motor zero offsets (d2, d3), scales (s1, s2, s3; s2 about θ2 = 45°), axis tilts (M2 vs M1, M3 vs M2) and camera tilt (the camera axis vs M3). Each alignment has its own rotation (the tripod and leveling change); terms are fitted unweighted by least squares on the angular residual.

Validation: leave one alignment out. Shared terms come from the other alignments; the held-out one gets only a rotation.

  • The M2 scale is a Polaris property: +1–2% on every mount, tightest on Mark's (0.6–1.3% over 11 nights). With the 300 mm lens it was +2.0% against +1.0% for the 200 mm on the same mount, so it may be partly gravity sag (an error growing towards the horizon, roughly cos(alt)), which over 20–70° looks like a scale and offset.
  • The offsets belong to the setup: jdm 200 mm about +50′ / −53′ on three nights; 300 mm +110′ / −2′; Mark's M3 offset −5°, +6° or ~0 on different nights (the M3 zero reference or the camera's rotation in its clamp changing between sessions).
  • Terms fitted on the 200 mm nights help other setups (e.g. 300 mm: 113′ → 64′) but are not enough; the offsets must be fitted per alignment.

Design¶

Model¶

θ' = θ + (0, d2 + s2·(θ2 − 45°), d3)          corrected motor angles (after the worm feed-forward)
cameraQ = alignQ_B2T · theta_to_q(θ')          then sync guide, pulse guide, SCC, roll_adj as now
  • Per alignment (refitted at every sync, with the rotation): alignQ_B2T, d2, d3.
  • Mount constant (config, default +1.0%; refined by a calibration from many points): s2.
  • Inverse (commands, reachability): θ = θ' − (0, d2 + s2·(θ2' − 45°), d3) solved per motor (linear in θ2).

The terms act on the motor angles, so the corrected chain stays a pure rotation of theta_to_q: PID, KF, reach limits and the speed controller keep working in θ space, with one conversion at the boundary.

Fit¶

  • Joint least squares over the rotation and d2, d3 on the angular residual: unweighted (or robust, e.g. Huber), not proximity-weighted. The current recency, proximity (σ 10°) and polar weights let a rotation-only model fit locally around the target; with the physical terms that compensation should no longer be needed. Slew & Center Correction and sync guiding stay for the remaining local error.
  • Fewer than 3 points, or points without a spread in roll (both signs) and altitude: fit the rotation only, with d2, d3 held at the last values (or a weak prior towards them), so a single sync cannot produce wild offsets.
  • QUEST's closed form gives the starting rotation; a few Gauss–Newton steps refine all five parameters.

Cost¶

The fit runs only when an alignment sync adds a point or a setting forces a refit (worm on/off, site latitude): a handful of times a night. Sync-guiding syncs return before QUEST (sync_az_alt) and are unaffected. At every control tick the change is adding the offsets to the motor angles before theta_to_q: nothing measurable.

Measured on 2026-10-11 with the M11 alignment (10 points), the driver running on each machine:

The fit is numpy only, vectorised over the points, with analytic derivatives and a few Gauss–Newton steps from QUEST's closed-form rotation; the per-tick correction is a closed form, Rz(a1) Ry(a2′) Rx(−d3) Ry(−a2) Rz(−a1), instead of building the forward kinematics twice (63 µs on a desktop). Both meet the Pi Zero 2 W target (under ~20 ms per fit), so the fit runs inline at the sync, like QUEST did: running it off the event loop (asyncio.to_thread) isn't needed, and on a Pi would gain little anyway (small numpy operations hold the GIL).

Validation¶

  1. Offline replay (done): archived alignments above.
  2. Twin tests: inject known d2, d3, s2 into the digital twin; the fit recovers them, and gotos and tracking are unchanged with zero terms.
  3. On the mount:
    • the alignment RMS on the standard 6 points, before and after;
    • first-goto error on targets away from the sync points;
    • PEC drift rate on a long track (expected to shrink with the alignment error);
    • a scale or sag check: syncs at low and high altitude in one session, with the 200 mm and the 300 mm.

Open questions¶

  • Scale or sag? A scale is constant; sag depends on the load and grows towards the horizon. If it is sag, model cos(θ2) instead and store it per lens.
  • Mark's ±6° M3 offsets: is the M3 zero reference re-set by the firmware at power-on, or was the camera rotated? The 517 MCU angles (zeta) could tell, since they are logged with each point now.
  • Refraction: check whether the observed Az/Alt are refracted; about 1–3′ below 30°, small next to the terms above.
  • The remaining 2–8′: plate-solve error, backlash at the sync, worm error at the sync (≤ 1.7′), M2 slips.
  • Focuser pull and lens flexure: a constant sideways pull on the lens (e.g. the focuser) is indistinguishable from an M3 zero offset, since M3 turns the camera about its own up axis, so it is part of d3. If the pull comes from the focuser's weight bending the lens, it depends on gravity (roll and altitude) and is flexure: test a droop term along the gravity-down direction in the joint fit, and an alignment with the focuser removed.
