Goto Response — Theory, Measurements & Design for Flips, Unwinds and Goto Paths¶

Branch: feature/equatorial_mode (Oct 2026). Started from eq mode struggling with simple gotos (a goto to the zenith from Sydney turned M1 a full turn down and back up), it became a study of how gotos should move in both alt/az and eq mode.

Read Status and findings (2026-10-15) first. The sections after it hold the theory, the digital twin sweep and the hardware measurements it rests on.

Status and findings (2026-10-15)¶

What is built¶

  • Zenith fix (control.py prevent_windup): in eq mode the unwind check judges M1/M3 travel by the target's motor angles, not by the change in sky Az/Roll (on a wedge M1/M3 aren't the Az/Roll axes; near the zenith Az swings ~90 deg for a few deg of M1). Twin test test_goto_to_the_zenith_does_not_unwind.
  • utility/goto_response.py: a fixed sequence of 10 motor-angle gotos (⅖/30/90 deg on M1, 5/25 on M2, 5/30 on M3, 5 and ~30 on all three), each out from a base pose and back, with log_position on and each leg marked in the log. --track runs them while tracking, --plan checks the moves without moving, --twin dry-runs in the twin. M2 is kept within 5-70 deg and every target well inside the motor limits; Ctrl+C aborts.
  • utility/analyse_goto_response.ipynb (helpers in analyse_helpers.py): per run, leg and motor, the theoretical minimum time, arrival, commanded vs measured speed, response delay, overshoot, settling, and the Kd analysis below.
  • driver/control_plan.py (2026-10-17): the goto planning module (R3). GotoTrajectory, MotorTrajectory and ramp_rates moved out of control.py unchanged; min_move_time added. kinematics.theta_solutions lists every motor solution of a pose within the limits.
  • tests/test_control_plan.py: the twin sweep as a test, with the acceptance criteria as xfail tests (see the baseline).
  • Straight motor moves for idle slews over 15 deg (control_plan.MotorMove, choose_end_solution), and the LIMIT fix: see step 3.
  • utility/analyse_span.ipynb eq sections: reachability, PA (upright and upside down), the tripod (now the M3 disk model, R1) and latitude.

2 idle, battery 7.3 s 77' -
3 tracking, battery 8.9 s 1' small moves, 115-150' large +5.8 s
4 tracking, charging (59 -> 69%) 10.1 s 1' small moves, 115-190' large +6.4 s

  1. The motors follow their commands (measured / commanded speed 0.96-1.07 on large moves, all runs), whether one motor moves or all three: the independence assumption holds in these runs, on battery as well as charging.
  2. No measurable battery effect between runs, but the motors run faster than calibrated: M1 peaks at 10-11 deg/s against 8.92, M2 at 8-10 against 7.66 (the calibration underestimates by 10-25% at 49-69%). The battery % is unreliable around plugging in and out (the charging logic), so no conclusion on charge level.
  3. Idle gotos are planned but always overshoot: about 1-1.5 deg on every move, even 2 deg ones. The measured speed lags the command by 0.2-0.4 s (dead time, including the ~0.2 s age of a position sample); the plan doesn't allow for it, the motor falls 1-3 deg behind the plan, the PID pushes to full Kv, and the motor brakes late.

  4. Gotos while tracking aren't planned: the reference steps toward the target (up to 12 deg along the sky path, alpha_limit_step) and the PID chases it.

    • Small moves close as a first-order decay with τ ≈ 2 s: a 2 deg move that could take 1.3 s takes 12-13 s.
    • The Kd term sets that τ. With coordinated speed control ω_kd = -Kd (ω_fb - ω_ff) damps the PID's own feedback command back toward the feed-forward (the tracking rate), so ω_fb = Kp e / (1 + Kd): effective gain 1/(1 + Kd) (measured 0.65 and 0.57 on M1 and M3 against 0.67 and 0.56), time constant (1 + Kd)/Kp = 1.5 / 1.5 / 1.8 s, lengthened to ~2 s by the dead time and Ke smoothing. 5 deg to 1' is ln(300) τ ≈ 11 s.
    • Large single-motor moves reach full speed (the 12 deg lead) but have no braking plan: 2-3 deg overshoot.
    • Combined moves are slow because the lead is measured along the sky path: all_30 (M1 +30, M2 +25, M3 +30) is Az +84, Alt +12, Roll -47 in the sky; the 12 deg lead spread over three motors keeps each one's error, and so its speed, small (1-5 deg/s instead of ~8). 27 s for a move that could take 6 s.
    • Settling from 1' to the tracking tolerance (Kc/20 ≈ 2.3") takes another ~6 s, with BLC pushes in it.
    • A goto can complete while still moving: on m1_2_back (run 1) M1 passed through the target at ~0.5 deg/s, was within 0.75' for one tick, completed, went IDLE and coasted 11' past.

Plan from here¶

See Design. In order of what they gain: plan gotos while tracking too; compensate the dead time; complete only once stopped; choose the end solution once per goto for both modes; then tune the final settle (Kc/20, BLC). Throughout: the requirements - no path into the tripod cone, gotos interruptible from a moving state, new code in control_plan.py / kinematics.py reusing what exists, with FAST speed calibration re-enabled, and planning that never blocks the 200 ms tick on a Pi Zero 2 W or N1505.

Background¶

How a goto's motor path is decided today:

  1. q_to_theta picks one of the two IK branches (normal, or flipped: M1 ±180, -M2, M3 ∓180), whichever is nearer LastPosition, with a gimbal lock fallback.
  2. plan_goto (coordinated speed control) follows a trajectory in sky space (Az/Alt when idle, RA/Dec/PA while tracking), giving up to the PID where the path needs a branch change; large moves while tracking aren't planned.
  3. prevent_windup runs, every tick and in this order, UNWIND (the implied raw angle past a limit: ±360), EQ ROOM (eq mode: M1 turned for tracking room), LOCK/FLIP (a path through gimbal lock: straight to the motor angles) and FLIP (the target > 30 deg away in M1 or M3: cache it).

Each heuristic estimates motor travel its own way, some from sky angles, and they can override one another mid-goto.

Theory¶

A pose has a small, finite set of motor solutions. M1-M2-M3 is a three-axis wrist: for one camera orientation there are two IK branches (normal, and flipped: M1 ±180, -M2, M3 ∓180), but with M2's range -8 to 81.5 deg both are valid only where |M2| ≤ 8 (the flipped branch needs -M2 in range too): outside that 16 deg slice by gimbal lock a pose has one branch. Each solution can also have M1 and M3 a whole turn either way. So the choice is almost always the M1/M3 turns, and the branch only in the slice. It is the same on a wedge: the wedge only changes the base -> sky rotation (alignQ_B2T), not the motors' geometry.

With independent motors the fastest move to a chosen solution is known exactly. If each motor has its own speed and acceleration limit and doesn't constrain the others (confirmed in the measurements above), the minimum time is the slowest single motor's rest-to-rest time (accelerate, cruise, decelerate). Nothing beats it, and a coordinated straight line in motor angles achieves it.

A straight motor-space move can't cross a motor limit: the limits form a box and a straight line between two points inside a box stays inside it. Gimbal lock is no obstacle to a motor-space move either; it's a singularity of the sky -> motor conversion, not of the motors.

So an ideal free move is a discrete choice of end solution, then a straight, time-optimal motor move to it.

Where that needs more:

  • Constrained paths: holding Az/Alt through a roll change, or RA/Dec through a PA change, can't be faster than a free move. Following a fixed path as fast as each motor's limits allow is time-optimal path following (Bobrow; Shin & McKay, 1985), which GotoTrajectory approximates. Constrain the path when what the camera sees during the move matters (a rotation on the same target, small corrections); slews between targets move freely.
  • A rotation must keep the pointing, or the pose is lost: M3 turns the camera about camera-up (the M3 axis), so M3 alone pans the boresight - a roll or PA change is never a single-motor move but M1, M2 and M3 together, and a straight motor-space move to the rotated pose swings the pointing far from the Az/Alt or RA/Dec on the way. Rotations (and the rotation part of a goto) follow the pointing-held path.
  • Constraints that aren't motor limits: eq mode's rig tilt, the camera or cables against the tripod, the Sun. These can rule out straight lines.
  • What else matters than speed: tracking room (below) and cable wrap (M1 and M3 turning the same way) decide between end solutions.
  • Dead time: with a 0.2-0.4 s delay between command and measured response, a controller chasing the measured position overshoots. Compare against the plan delayed by the dead time (or predict the motor forward), and brake that much earlier.

Tracking room: after a goto, tracking keeps turning the motors for hours (M1 at ~15 deg/h in eq mode; M1 and M3 in alt/az). The same pointing reached at M1 +200 or -160 tracks identically, but if tracking drives M1 upward the first leaves 60 deg (~4 h) before a 360 deg unwind mid-session and the second 420 deg. Choosing by tracking room is what EQ ROOM does for M1 in eq mode.

Step 3: straight motor moves (2026-10-17)¶

Idle gotos whose pointing moves more than 15 deg now go straight in motor angles (control_plan.MotorMove): each motor on its own closed-form time-optimal profile from its current angle and speed, the end solution chosen once at the start (choose_end_solution: within the limits, eq tracking room, no cable wrap on a flip, then the fastest), the reference the plan as the measurements should show it (see below) so the PID doesn't push against the lag, and the goto timeout extended by the planned time. Redirected mid-move, it re-plans from the current speeds. Flips past gimbal lock and EQ ROOM turns use the same MotorMove (MotorTrajectory is gone). Gotos while tracking, and idle ones of 15 deg or less, are unchanged so far.

Also fixed: LIMIT mode left the motors running at their last speed. control() sent no commands in LIMIT and no stop either (the mount still counted as moving): in the twin M2 swung on to 157 deg and M1 ran at full speed; on the mount that drives into the mechanical stop. Entering LIMIT now stops every motor, and they stay stopped until the alarm is acknowledged (test_guard_limit_stops_every_motor).

Design¶

  1. Choose the end solution once, at the start of the goto, for both modes. Make the target pose reachable (below), list its motor solutions (M1/M3 turns, and the second branch only when the target's |M2| ≤ 8), drop those outside the motor limits or (eq) past the rig tilt on the way; score the rest lexicographically: never a limit, then tracking room up to a night (8 h, both modes: project the target's sky motion), then the move time, with cable wrap (M1/M3 the same way) as a penalty. Fix the end angles for the goto; nothing re-decides mid-goto. This replaces UNWIND / EQ ROOM / LOCK / FLIP during a goto; prevent_windup stays as the guard while tracking.

  2. Plan every goto, tracking ones too: a time-optimal trajectory with feed-forward to where the target will be on arrival, then hand over to tracking. Removes the PID chase (τ ≈ 2 s) and the sky-path lead on combined moves. The path depends on how far the pointing moves (agreed 2026-10-17):

    • Pointing moves 15 deg or less (goto_controlled_path_deg, the angle between the two pointings) and every roll/PA-only change: a controlled pointing path. The pointing travels the great circle from start to target (RA/Dec while tracking), and roll/PA changes in proportion to progress along it, so the rotation never swings the pointing off that natural path (a straight motor move would: M3 alone pans the pointing). As fast as the motors allow along that path (path following, GotoTrajectory today). If the twin sweep shows these moves much slower than straight ones, allow the pointing a small corridor around the great circle (e.g. 1 deg or a few % of the move) for the motors to cut corners.
    • Pointing moves more than 15 deg: fast and straight in motor angles, closed-form per motor from the current state; the path through the sky isn't controlled. Path checks (tripod cone, rig tilt) apply here; a controlled path of 15 deg or less between targets above the horizon stays within 15 deg of them and can't reach the cone.
    • Fallback: a controlled path that needs an IK branch change or passes the motor limits goes straight instead.
    • Redirects mid-goto (R2) can switch between the two, both starting from the current motor speeds.
    • Compensate the dead time (0.2-0.4 s): feedback against the plan delayed by the dead time, braking that much earlier. Removes the ~1-1.5 deg overshoot.
    • Complete a goto only once the motors have stopped, not on one tick within tolerance.
    • Goto timeout from the planned time plus a margin, not a fixed 45 s.
    • Then tune the final settle: Kc/20 while tracking and BLC pushes, once the overshoot is gone.

Requirements (2026-10-16)¶

The design must also meet these three requirements.

R1. Tripod collision prevention.

Superseded 2026-10-17 (user's model). The tripod is not a cone round the boresight's nadir. The camera and OTA sit square to the M3 axis, so spinning M3 sweeps the OTA - front and rear - round a disk square to the M3 axis, set by M1 and M2 alone; on a wedge that disk can reach the tripod when M2 tilts the M3 axis towards the horizon (worst with the M2 axis across the meridian; with it in the meridian plane the disk passes down the side of the tripod). The tripod is the solid cone its legs span (spreader, tray, head between them), not three lines. Modelled in utility/analyse_span.ipynb (Tripod collision: the M3 disk: 3D rig, M1 x M2 / M3 x M2 maps, and a sky map of the rolls the tripod takes: Sydney, front 1.0 / rear 0.5 / legs 20 deg: ~10% of reachable Az/Alt/roll above the horizon, none clear round Alt 60-82, Az 150-195 - the rear; very sensitive to the rear length). The boresight cone below and the rig-tilt limit (rig_tilt_deg, limit_rig_tilt, eq_rig_tilt_limit, the R1 acceptance test) are removed from the driver; no tripod protection is enforced until the disk check is designed (needs the rig's measured rear length and leg splay). The original text follows for the record.

The tripod fills the cone around the nadir: altitude -81.5 to -90 deg, at any azimuth. On a level mount the motors can't reach it: M2's 81.5 deg limit holds the boresight within -81.5 to +81.5 deg altitude, so this mode is unaffected. A wedge tilts that range: M2's limit is now measured from the M1 axis (the pole), and the nadir comes within reach.

  • Which poses. The clearest description is in sky terms, by two directions of the pose:

    • boresight nadir distance: the angle from the nadir to where the lens points (= 90 + boresight altitude). The tripod cone is nadir distance < 8.5 deg (tripod_cone_deg, new; 8.5 is the level mount's own margin, so alt/az poses never trip it; set it larger from the rig's measured clearance for long lenses).
    • rig tilt: the angle of camera-up (the M3 axis) from the zenith. This is rig_tilt_deg and eq_rig_tilt_limit, which already exist (the rig hanging under the Polaris).

    These are separate constraints. Every pose in the tripod cone has a rig tilt of 81.5-98 deg (camera-up is roughly horizontal when the lens points down), so eq_rig_tilt_limit ≤ 81.5 would exclude the cone too, but at a cost of much of the PA range near the poles. The tripod cone is the narrow constraint that must always apply.

  • In motor terms (Sydney wedge, lat -33.9, computed from the driver's kinematics on a 5 deg grid): 0.46% of motor space points into the cone. Every such pose has |M2| ≥ ~25 deg and M1 + M3 within about ±40 deg of 180. Few end poses are lost, but a straight motor-angle move between two clear poses can pass through it.

  • What the planner must do. Every planned path, braking and interrupted paths included (R2), is checked for boresight nadir distance (and rig tilt) along its length, not only at its end. If a path enters the cone, the planner tries the other end solutions (item 1). If none is clear, it adds a waypoint around the cone. If no route is clear, the goto is refused with a warning; the planner never clips mid-move. A target inside the cone goes to the nearest pose on the cone's edge, as reachability does for other targets out of reach.
  • Tracking into the cone (a target setting below the tripod) stops at its edge, as at the motor limits.

R2. Fully interruptible gotos. A goto in progress can be redirected mid-flight: a new SlewTo/target, a sync, a tracking change, AbortSlew, or an orbital's moving setpoint.

  • Replan from the current state, not from rest. Each motor's position and velocity (the measured θ_pv and ω, and the commands still in flight during the dead time, i.e. the state predicted one dead time ahead) are the start of the new trajectory. A motor moving away from the new target first brakes within Ka, and that braking time and distance count in the plan.
  • The end solution is chosen again for the new target, scored from that moving state. An interruption is a new goto: "choose once" applies per goto, never mid-goto for the same target. Reversing a fast-moving M1 can cost more than a slightly worse solution in its direction of travel, and the scoring has to see that.
  • No step in the commands. The speed commands stay continuous across the replan (within Ka), and the dead-time compensation (item 3) carries over.
  • Abort is the same planner with "stop" as the target: a planned stop within Ka, not a hard stop. Afterwards the goto completes only once stopped (item 4).
  • GotoTrajectory already replans "from the current speed" on retarget (per path, not per motor); build on that rather than writing a new planner.

    • Budget: a tick's planning work under 10% of the tick (20 ms) on the minimum hardware, with no single tick over 50 ms of planning. The control tick itself is unchanged.
    • Never block the tick for a plan. Plan in slices across ticks, or in a worker (asyncio.to_thread). Under the GIL a thread still competes for the CPU unless the work is in numpy, and a separate process costs memory a 512 MB Pi can't spare. Until the new plan is ready the mount keeps following the current trajectory, or brakes within Ka. The new plan starts from the state predicted for its handover tick (R2), so a plan taking one or two ticks costs no pause.
    • Do less work: closed-form trapezoid/triangle profiles per motor for straight motor-angle moves (Design item 2's slews: no path sampling at all). Path sampling only for the pointing-held paths (rotations, small corrections), with the samples shared by every check that needs them (gimbal lock, tripod cone R1, rig tilt, the trajectory), not one pass each.
    • Vectorised kinematics: IK/FK over an array of poses in one numpy call, in kinematics.py (R3), in place of one Quaternion per sample in Python.
    • Cross-platform: pure Python and numpy/scipy (already dependencies). No compiled extensions or OS-specific code in the planner (numba and the like don't install cleanly on a Pi Zero or every Windows Python).
    • Measure it: one log line per goto with the planning time and the number of ticks it spanned, so field logs show it alongside the heartbeat lags; a twin test that counts planning operations per goto, not wall time, so it is stable on any machine; a benchmark run on a Pi Zero 2 W and on the N1505 before release.

Poses, reachability and what is left to choose¶

Az/Alt/Roll is only how a pose is written, and it writes some poses twice and is singular at the zenith - hence the wrap and flip rules in reachable_azaltroll (kinematics.py). In the design a target becomes a camera orientation (quaternion) once, and the rules separate into:

  • Altitude over the top (vertoramas, poles): alt 120 at az is alt 60 at az + 180 with roll + 180 - the same orientation written differently. Nothing to choose: its motor solutions are the same.
  • Upside down (roll ± 180, the same footprint): not a choice. Camera-up is the M3 axis and M2 keeps it within 81.5 deg of the M1 axis (the zenith on a level base, the pole on a wedge); up and its opposite are 180 deg apart, so they can't both be inside that cone. Hence altitude_to_maxroll ≤ 81.5 deg and roll and roll ± 180 are never both reachable: reachability decides which one the frame gets, though M3 itself can turn fully.
  • Reachability (M2 and M3): the reachable poses are those with a motor solution inside the limits; altitude_to_maxroll's cos(roll) = cos(θ2max)/cos(alt) is that boundary written in sky angles. A target outside it goes to the nearest reachable pose with today's priority - pointing first, then roll (the zenith zone: directly below on the same azimuth) - in motor space, which also covers eq mode, where _reachable_alpha now goes through the base frame and back.
  • Continuity: a target tracked through the zenith zone keeps its side (test_zenith_transit.py) - choosing once and holding the solution, not re-choosing as the target moves.

So per goto the choices are the M1/M3 turns, the branch in the |M2| ≤ 8 slice, and the nearest reachable pose when the target is out of reach. Panorama panels are gotos like any other (the driver doesn't control their timing); the existing pano, reachability and zenith transit tests (test_pano_wrap.py, test_reachable_azaltroll.py, test_zenith_transit.py) define the behaviour to keep.

Eq mode reachability, PA and upside-down frames (2026-10-17)¶

From utility/analyse_span.ipynb (eq sections; vector maths checked against the driver's reachable_azaltroll, theta_to_q and q_to_theta). PA is the driver's: camera-up from the direction of the north celestial pole.

  • Motor reach on a wedge depends on Dec and PA only, not hour angle. The rule is the level mount's (camera-up within 81.5 deg of the M1 axis) with the M1 axis on the pole; tracking turns M1 about the pole and leaves camera-up's angle to it unchanged. So a target that is reachable at some PA stays reachable at that PA all night, as far as the motors go (the rig tilt limit and the tripod cone depend on hour angle).

  • Upright, camera-up must lean toward the visible pole: in the south, |PA| beyond about 98 deg at Dec 0. Upside down (PA + 180, the same footprint) covers the centre band, |PA| below about 82 deg. Neither works where camera-up would be within 8.5 deg of square to the pole (PA near ±90 at Dec 0); that gap widens toward the poles until nothing is left beyond |Dec| 81.5 (the polar caps):

    Dec Upright Upside down only Either
    0 45% 45% 91%
    -45 43% 43% 86%
    -75 31% 31% 62%
    -85 0% 0% 0%

  • Allowing upside down exactly doubles the reachable PA, at every latitude and every rig tilt limit (sky above the horizon: 43% -> 87% with motor reach alone). A PA and its upside-down twin are never both reachable (camera-up and camera-down can't both be within 81.5 deg of the M1 axis, nor both within a tilt limit of the zenith), so the flip only ever adds PAs. This is the "upside down is not a choice" rule above, seen in eq terms: for any requested framing at most one of the two is reachable, and reachability decides which.

  • What the driver does today: reachable_azaltroll tries the upside-down frame first and clamps to the nearest reachable roll only if neither is reachable; eq mode applies it in the base frame (_reachable_alpha). With eq_rig_tilt_limit set, limit_rig_tilt then takes the smallest allowed PA turn, which can be a nearer edge than the flipped frame (see Open questions).

  • The rig tilt limit costs PA, most near the equator (where the M1 axis is nearly horizontal). Mean share of PA reachable over the sky above the horizon, upright / either way up:

    Latitude Motor reach alone Rig tilt ≤ 100 Rig tilt ≤ 81.5
    5 43 / 86% 27 / 54% 20 / 40%
    35 43 / 87% 35 / 70% 28 / 56%
    70 43 / 87% 43 / 86% 38 / 75%

  • Tripod cone (R1): no end pose above the horizon is affected at any latitude. Straight motor-angle gotos between poses above 10 deg enter the cone in 0.9% of cases at Sydney, about 1.2% near the equator and none from about 50 deg latitude (the wedge nearly level). Every one first tips the rig past 81.5 deg, and those gotos swing M1 and M3 a long way (median 159 and 162 deg against 90 and 76 for all gotos). The rig tilt passes 100 deg partway through 43% of straight gotos, so with eq_rig_tilt_limit set the path check matters far more for the rig tilt than for the cone.

Tracking in eq mode¶

With QUEST's alignment matching the real wedge, tracking turns the pose about the true pole and the IK spreads it over all three motors: M1 at the sidereal rate, M2 and M3 correcting in proportion to the wedge's error (computed from the driver's kinematics, 4 h from reachable eq poses):

Wedge error M1 over 4 h M2 over 4 h M3 over 4 h Peak M2/M3 rate
none 60.16 deg (sidereal) 0 0 0
0.5 deg 59.9-60.8 deg 4-25' 4-45' 4-14'/h
2 deg 59.0-62.6 deg 17-100' 16-182' 16-55'/h
  • M3 takes out the field rotation a misaligned wedge leaves, which a two-axis equatorial mount can't.
  • M2/M3 creep at ~0.1"/s, far below the slowest SLOW speed (~21"/s): start-stop pulses (the speed controller's PWM), reversing direction during a night, where backlash takes up (BLC).
  • Tracking room in eq mode is M1's: M2/M3 use a few degrees a night at most.
  • QUEST needs syncs spread over the sky to see the pole axis error (its test uses four); with fewer, tracking drifts at about the error × the sidereal rate.

Orbitals¶

Orbitals can be tracked (advanced_orbitals, tracking rates 1-3 in orbital2delta) but never stayed on target well at longer focal lengths. That is more a tracking than a goto problem, but the two meet:

  • How it works now: every tick the setpoint is the orbital's RA/Dec computed 2.5 s ahead. While the orbital is below 10 deg altitude the setpoint isn't updated and the mount tracks sidereal where it is; once it is above, the setpoint jumps to the orbital - an unplanned goto while tracking, chased by the PID (τ ≈ 2 s, overshoot on large moves), onto a target that keeps moving.
  • A moving target needs its velocity (and acceleration) in the feed-forward: a PID that closes errors with τ ≈ 2 s lags a target moving at rate v by about v × τ unless the feed-forward carries the motion. The fixed 2.5 s lead should match the loop's real lag instead - the measured dead time is 0.2-0.4 s - and at 1 deg/s every 0.1 s of mismatch is 6'. Done 2026-10-20 (orbital_lead_sec, default 0.3 s), after the mount led NORAD 45417 live.
  • PEC (worm feed-forward) and BLC have since addressed periodic error and backlash, two of the problems that made orbitals hard; what's left is the moving-target feed-forward, the dead time and planning the acquisition: a planned goto to where the orbital will be on arrival (design item 2), then tracking with its rate.
  • An orbital response test like goto_response.py could measure this without the sky: follow a recorded or synthetic pass (in the twin, then on the mount in daylight) and compare θ_sp with θ_pv.

Acceptance criteria¶

  • Twin sweep (to become a test: a seeded subset in the suite, the full sweep marked slow): no LIMIT, no mid-goto remap, M2 never out of range, chosen end solution within 2 s of T_best except where tracking room decides, median arrival within 1.3× T_best.
  • Hardware, goto_response.py idle and --track: median arrival - theoretical minimum under 3 s; overshoot under 5' on moves ≤ 5 deg and under 15' on large ones; no completion while moving.
  • Tripod cone (R1): in the twin sweep (eq mode, several latitudes) no sample of any planned path, braking included, has a boresight nadir distance under tripod_cone_deg.
  • Interruption (R2): twin tests that redirect gotos at random times mid-flight show continuous speed commands (no step beyond Ka), no LIMIT, and arrival within 1.3× the T_best of a plan from the moving state.
  • Platform performance (R4): in the twin, no tick does more than its planning budget of operations (none over 50 ms equivalent on a Pi Zero 2 W); on a Pi Zero 2 W and the N1505, no heartbeat lag over 0.15 s from planning across a goto_response.py run.
  • The existing test suite passes, including the pano, reachability and zenith transit tests.
